    Common Ground

    Every verse of seven founding texts, placed by what it means. Each verse was read by one multilingual language model and turned into a point in a space where nearby points say similar things; that space was flattened to a map, and the map drawn as ground whose height is how many verses stand there. Where the colours pile up, the traditions say the same thing. A region of one colour is a tradition's own.

    What the numbers mean

    Kinship is the share of a verse's fifteen nearest neighbours that come from other texts, divided by the share a shuffle would give its text, so a small text is not counted as shared just for being small. Nearest in each other text is the closest verse by meaning in each of the six other texts. Search by meaning embeds what you type with the same model, in your browser, and scores every verse against it; the model is fetched once and nothing you type leaves the page.

    What to be careful of

    These are English translations, so part of the geometry belongs to the translators. The unit of text differs: a Bible verse, a Quranic aya, a stanza of the Gita, a chapter of the Analects. The model is small and the map is one run of a layout algorithm; closeness of meaning is not agreement, and a neighbour is the nearest verse, not a matching one. Treat what you find as a place to start reading, not a conclusion.
