    Common Ground

    Seven scriptures, one map: every verse placed by what it says. A language model read each of thirty-four thousand verses and gave it a place, so that verses which say similar things sit close together. That space was flattened to a map, and the map raised into ground whose height is how many verses stand there. Where the colours meet, the traditions say similar things. Where one colour stands alone, a tradition speaks for itself.

    What the numbers mean

    Shared ground asks how many of a verse's fifteen nearest neighbours belong to other texts, against what chance would give its own text, so a small text is not called shared just for being small. It describes the verse, not any pair. Nearest in each other text is the closest verse by meaning in each of the six other texts. The bar beside each shows how close the pair is, in fifths set against every such pair in the corpus; the weakest third are left out, so a verse that keeps its own company may list only a few, or none. Search by meaning reads what you type with the same model, in your browser, and scores every verse against it.

    What to be careful of

    These are English translations, so part of the map belongs to the translators, and for some of these texts the translation is not the scripture. The units differ: a Bible verse, an aya of the Quran, a stanza of the Gita, a chapter of the Analects. The model is small and the layout is one run of one algorithm. Closeness is of wording and subject, not of belief: two verses can sit side by side and disagree, and a nearest verse is not a matching one. Treat what you find as a place to start reading, not a conclusion.

    The seven texts are a starting set, chosen because open editions exist, not a ranking or a canon; others belong here and may follow. "Hebrew Bible" names the thirty-nine books as the King James Version orders them. The region names are one reader's captions for what each cluster mostly holds. The symbols mark which text a verse comes from and nothing more. If something here is wrong or reads badly, please say so.

    The texts

      How it works

      Each verse is turned into a list of 384 numbers by multilingual-e5-small, a small sentence-embedding model. Two verses that say similar things get similar lists, and their closeness is the cosine between them. UMAP flattens those 384 dimensions to two while keeping neighbours near each other; the height of the ground is the density of verses on that flat map, blurred. Regions are clusters on the map, named by hand from their most telling words. Every verse also carries its nearest verse in each other text, worked out once and stored.

      The page is a static site drawn with three.js: the verses are one cloud of points whose shader knows both their reading-order place and their place on the map, so the whole corpus flies between the two on one number. Search by meaning fetches the same model into your browser through transformers.js the first time you use it, embeds what you typed, and scores it against the stored embeddings of every verse; nothing you type leaves the page. The code is at github.com/shuw/common-ground.
